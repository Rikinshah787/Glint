Try Glint in two minutes

Each box below is a normal text field, just like Gmail or Slack. Glint works in any app — this page is a safe place to practise. Make sure Glint is running (look for the menu-bar icon).

1Autocomplete

Click the box, type the start of a sentence, then pause for a moment. A suggestion appears just under your text. Press Tab to accept it, Esc to dismiss, or just keep typing. ⌥Tab asks for one right away.

Nothing showing? Type at least a dozen characters and stop at the end of a line. Autocomplete stays off in code editors, terminals and password fields on purpose.

2Assist: instant replies

Dana (Slack)Hey! Could you take a look at the Q3 budget draft before Friday? Mainly the vendor costs section.

Click into the reply box and tap ⌥⌘ together (press and release). Glint drafts a reply from what's on your screen. Use Shorter or More formal, then insert it.

Hold ⌥⌘ instead to open Quick Ask; double-tap it for the side panel.

3Dictate

Click the box, then hold ⌃⌥ (Control + Option), say a sentence, and let go. Glint removes the "um"s, fixes punctuation and types it for you. Esc cancels.

The first time, macOS asks for microphone access — click Allow.

4Quick Ask: your memory

Press ⌘⇧K from any app and ask about anything you've seen or done: "what was I working on this morning?", "find the link Sam sent about pricing", or type /summarize. Press Esc to go back to where you were.

Glint remembers the text of the windows you work in (locally, on this Mac). The more you use your Mac, the more it can answer. Browse it all in Glint → Memory.

5Glance: explain my screen

Press ⌃⇧G on anything confusing — an error, a chart, a settings page — and a short explanation appears next to your cursor.

Needs Screen Recording permission (System Settings → Privacy & Security). Glint asks the first time.

Everything stays on your Mac unless you add a Claude or OpenAI key. Settings live in Glint → Settings.
